Rent or Buy Bengaluru

Four ways to live in the same flat: rent it and invest, buy it ready on a loan, buy it ready with cash, or book it under construction and move in at handover. Same cash out each month; every path invests whatever it doesn't spend; Indian taxes applied. Move any control; everything recomputes.

Location and flat

Why it matters

Presets fill the price per sq ft and a typical 3BHK rent for the area. They are October 2026 listing prices deflated by 22%, because asking prices in Bengaluru run about 29% above the transacted city average (₹12,400 vs ₹9,600). The ratio of rent to price (yield) is the location input that changes the answer most.

Why it matters

Rate × size = the flat price. Builders quote on super built-up area (your flat plus a share of lobbies and amenities), which is 20–30% more than the carpet area you live in.

Why it matters

Super built-up size. 2,000 sq ft at ₹10,000 is a ₹2 crore flat.

Price = rate × size. Edit either; the other follows.
Why it matters

Everything scales from this: down payment, loan, stamp duty, and the appreciation you earn. The under-construction path uses this price minus the launch discount.

A lifestyle upgrade is a higher rent here. ₹2 Cr flats rent at roughly 3.5–4% of price a year.
Why it matters

The rent is what the owner saves by owning. The real return on a home is the rent you stop paying; appreciation is the bonus. Set it to the flat you would actually live in if you rented, even a better one.

Bengaluru: ~12% a year 2020–25, ~0% 2014–20, +9% in H1 2026
Why it matters

The single assumption that decides buy vs rent. Nobody knows it. Use the sensitivity table below rather than one number.

Loan and purchase costs

Why it matters

Banks lend at most 75% above ₹75 lakh, so 25% is the minimum. More down means a smaller EMI and less interest, but less cash invested elsewhere. 100% means paying cash; see the loan-vs-cash box.

Why it matters

Floating rates in Oct 2026: SBI 7.25–8.55%, ICICI from 7.55%, HDFC from 7.75%. The loan rate is the hurdle your investments must beat for borrowing to make sense.

Why it matters

Longer tenure: lower EMI, more total interest, more cash free to invest. For the under-construction path the tenure starts at handover, so the loan ends later.

Karnataka, above ₹45L: 5% duty + 0.6% cess and surcharge + 2% registration ≈ 7.6%
Why it matters

Paid once, never recovered, and charged on the fully-built value even for an under-construction flat. On ₹2 Cr that is ₹15 lakh gone on day one.

Why it matters

Brokerage and paperwork when you sell. Only matters in the "if sold" value.

Under construction

Blogs quote 10–20% with no data. Listing portals show new launches priced above nearby resale in 15 of 16 localities, so check your project and set this yourself.
Why it matters

The discount is the main reason to buy before completion. Set it from the actual quote against a comparable ready flat nearby.

Why it matters

Until handover you pay rent plus interest on the disbursed loan. The longer the wait, the more rent and pre-EMI you pay, and the later your EMIs start.

63% of Bengaluru Urban RERA projects (2,784 of 4,406) were past their recorded completion date in Sept 2026; 1,422 had taken an extension.
Why it matters

A delay on its own costs little in this model when your investments earn more than the loan rate, because paying later is cheaper. The real risk of delay is a builder who never delivers, which no calculator can price. Treat long delays as a warning sign.

5% without input credit on most flats; 1% affordable; none on a ready flat with occupancy certificate
Why it matters

GST is the hidden cost of buying before completion: 5% of ₹1.8 Cr is ₹9 lakh that a ready flat does not charge. It eats half of a 10% launch discount.

Karnataka charges 0.5% on the agreement to sell at booking, credited against the sale deed, and the rest (to 7.6%) when the sale deed is registered, valued as if fully built. Payment plan: your down payment at booking, the bank's share disbursed evenly until handover, interest-only pre-EMI on what's disbursed (SBI, HDFC, ICICI), full EMI from handover with the tenure above. You keep paying rent until you move in.

Costs of owning

Why it matters

Kitchen, wardrobes, furniture. Paid on day one (ready) or at handover (under construction). Mostly worth nothing at resale; set the recovery below.

Why it matters

How much of the interiors spend a buyer pays you for. Usually 0–30%.

Why it matters

If a tenant would pay the society charge too, it cancels between the paths. If the landlord absorbs it, only the owner pays and it counts against buying.

Why it matters

Repainting, plumbing, appliances, the things a landlord pays for a tenant. 0.3% of price a year is ₹60,000 on a ₹2 Cr flat. Exclude to see the owner's costs without it.

Why it matters

Paid to the city by the owner only. ₹15,000–30,000 a year for a ₹2 Cr flat.

Why it matters

What a renter spends to make a flat liveable. Subtracted from the renter's day-one investment.

Why it matters

Rent rises with the city; an EMI does not. By year 15 at 5% growth the rent is double. This is the renter's hidden cost and the owner's hidden benefit.

Returns and horizon

Why it matters

What the money you don't put into the flat earns. 11% is a common index-fund assumption; 7% is a fixed deposit; 4% is idle savings. If you would not actually invest the difference, set this low and watch the answer change.

Why it matters

Buying has large one-time costs, so it needs years to catch up. Short horizons favour renting; long ones favour whichever asset compounds faster.

Tax

New regime (the default since FY 2023-24): no home-loan interest or principal deduction for a self-occupied home, and no HRA. Old regime: ₹2L interest (24b) and ₹1.5L principal (80C) per co-borrower after possession, plus HRA. From FY 2026-27 Bengaluru counts as a 50% HRA city under the old regime (Rule 279 of the 2026 Rules; confirm the final notification).
Why it matters

Under the new regime housing is tax-neutral, so the decision is purely economic. Under the old regime both sides get help: the buyer on the loan, the renter on HRA. Switching regimes changes your whole tax bill, not just housing, so compare regimes with your CA.

Why it matters

Co-owning with your spouse doubles the ₹1.25L a year tax-free equity gain and, under the old regime, the loan deductions. The property gain is also split between two returns.

Why it matters

The rate at which old-regime deductions save you money. 30% + 4% cess = 31.2% above ₹24 lakh.

Why it matters

Old-regime HRA exemption = the least of HRA received, rent paid minus 10% of basic, and 50% of basic (Bengaluru from FY 2026-27; 40% earlier). Leave both at zero to ignore it. Not available under the new regime.

Why it matters

Equity gains above ₹1.25L a year per person are taxed at 12.5%. Selling and rebuying up to that limit every year keeps those gains permanently untaxed. It is legal and takes ten minutes a year.

Why it matters

Gains on a home bought after July 2024 are taxed at 12.5% without indexation when you sell. Buying another home within the time limits (up to ₹10 Cr of gain) makes that zero. Choose "Yes" if you would roll into another home.

How the maths works

        
    
          
  • Each month the page finds the most expensive path and gives every path that same budget. Whatever a path doesn't spend, it invests at the return you set. One-time payments are paid from a path's own investments first; only the shortfall raises the shared budget.
    • 
          
  • Ready purchase: down payment, stamp duty and interiors on day one, then EMI plus owning costs. Value = flat value − loan − selling cost − 12.5% tax on the gain over cost (none with Section 54) + interiors recovered.
    • 
          
  • Under construction: booked at the launch price. Your down payment, GST on it and the 0.5% agreement duty go in at booking; the bank disburses its share evenly until handover and you pay interest only (pre-EMI) on what is disbursed, plus 5% GST on each instalment, while still paying rent. At handover (promise plus delay) you pay the balance of stamp duty and registration (or all of it at booking, your choice) and interiors; rent stops and the full EMI runs for the tenure you set. Banks do not publish when the contractual tenure clock starts, so this page starts it at handover. Before handover the flat's resale value is the ready price with the launch discount closing in a straight line.
    • 
          
  • Under construction versus ready: both end up owning the same flat, so price growth cancels between them. The under-construction path wins by the launch discount and by paying later (smaller, later loan; your spare cash invested meanwhile), and loses by the GST, the rent and pre-EMI paid while waiting, and a loan that runs past the ready buyer's. When your investments earn more than the loan rate, paying later is worth money, which is why a delay on its own barely hurts here. What makes delay dangerous is a builder who does not deliver, a risk this page cannot price. Treat long delays as a red flag, not a cost line.
    • 
          
  • Loan vs cash: the cash buyer pays the full price on day one; the loan buyer pays the down payment and invests the rest. The loan wins when the investment return exceeds the loan rate after tax; cash wins when it does not, or when you would not actually invest the difference.
    • 
          
  • Renting: furnishing on day one, then rent growing each year. Investments are taxed at 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25L per taxpayer in the year you cash out; harvesting lifts the cost basis by that exemption each year.
    • 
          
  • Old regime: tax saved on ₹2L of interest and ₹1.5L of principal per co-borrower after possession, and HRA for the renter (least of HRA received, rent minus 10% of basic, 50% of basic). New regime: nothing for either side. Pre-EMI interest under the old regime (claimable in five parts after possession) is not modelled.
    • 
          
  • Not modelled: moving out and renting the flat to a tenant, inflation in your spending, rate changes mid-loan, a builder who never delivers.
    •
